You open the door at the end of a wash and the drum is still full of grey water. Or the machine stops mid-cycle, shows an error code and refuses to spin. A washing machine that won't drain is one of the most common faults we're called out to across London and Surrey, and it's also one of the faults where a few careful checks at home can sometimes get you going again.

This guide walks through the checks that are safe for a householder to try, in the order we'd suggest doing them. It also explains when to stop and call an engineer, so you don't spend an afternoon on a fault that needs a replacement part.

## First, make the machine safe

Before you touch anything:

- **Switch the machine off and unplug it.** Water and electricity don't mix, and you'll be working near the bottom of the appliance.
- **Let the water cool.** If the machine stopped during a hot wash, the water inside can be hot enough to scald. Give it time.
- **Don't force the door.** Most machines keep the door locked while there's water in the drum or for a few minutes after a cycle ends. Forcing it can break the door lock and flood the floor.
- **Have towels and a shallow tray ready.** A baking tray or roasting tin works well, because there's very little clearance under the drain point.

## Check 1: Is it really a drain fault?

Some settings deliberately leave water in the drum or skip the spin. Check whether a **rinse hold**, **no spin** or **delicates** option was selected, or whether the cycle simply hasn't finished. Run a short **drain** or **spin** programme on its own and see if the water goes.

It's also worth thinking about the load. A single heavy item, such as a bath mat or a duvet, can throw the drum off balance. Many machines will then refuse to spin to protect themselves, and the clothes come out soaking wet even though the water has drained. That's an unbalanced load rather than a drainage fault.

## Check 2: Clean the pump filter

The pump filter (sometimes called the debris filter or coin trap) is the most common culprit, and the one you can most easily sort out yourself. It catches the things that escape from pockets before they reach the drain pump: coins, hair grips, buttons, tissues, underwire from bras and the occasional small sock.

On most front-loading machines the filter sits behind a small flap or kickplate at the bottom front corner. Check your manual for the exact position on your model.

1. Place your tray and towels under the flap. Expect more water than you think. A full drum can hold several litres.
2. If there's a small emergency drain hose next to the filter, pull it out, remove its cap and let the water run into the tray. Re-cap it whenever the tray fills up.
3. Once the flow stops, unscrew the filter slowly, anticlockwise. More water will come out.
4. Clear out any debris and rinse the filter under the tap.
5. With a torch, look into the opening. If you can see the pump impeller, check it turns freely and isn't jammed by something caught behind the filter.
6. Screw the filter back in firmly and straight, so it doesn't leak.

Then plug the machine back in and run a drain or rinse-and-spin programme. If the water now pumps away, you've likely found the problem.

> **Tip:** cleaning the filter every couple of months helps prevent this fault and keeps the machine smelling fresher. Check pockets before every wash.

## Check 3: Look at the drain hose

Pull the machine out carefully, without straining the hoses or the power cable, and follow the grey drain hose from the back of the machine to where it empties.

- **Kinks and crushing.** A machine pushed back against the wall can easily fold the hose flat.
- **Blockages.** If you can safely disconnect it, flush the hose through with water to check it's clear.
- **Height and position.** The hose should loop up and then down into the standpipe or sink connection. If it's pushed too far into the standpipe, water can siphon out during the wash. That's a different problem, but worth fixing while you're there.

## Check 4: Check the sink waste or standpipe

If your machine drains into the trap under the kitchen sink, the blockage may not be in the machine at all.

- **A brand-new sink trap or a newly installed machine?** Check whether the blanking cap or the small seal inside the trap's spigot was removed when the machine was connected. If it wasn't, the water has nowhere to go. This is a surprisingly common cause after a kitchen fit or a machine replacement.
- **Is the sink itself draining slowly?** Grease and food waste in the trap will also stop the washing machine draining. Clearing the sink trap often fixes both.
- **Standpipe drains** can block with lint over time. If the standpipe overflows when the machine pumps, the blockage is in your household plumbing rather than the appliance.

## Check 5: Too much detergent?

Too much detergent, or ordinary washing-up liquid or hand-wash products used by mistake, can create so many suds that the machine struggles to drain and spin. Some machines will pause the programme or show a suds warning.

If you open the door to a drum full of foam, run a rinse cycle with no detergent at all, and use the dose recommended on the pack next time. Most packs give different doses for soft and hard water, and for lightly or heavily soiled loads, so it's worth checking which applies to you.

## Check 6: Note the error code

Most modern machines display a code when they can't drain. The code alone won't tell you exactly what's broken, but it's very useful to an engineer. Note it down, along with the make and model number (usually on a sticker inside the door rim), before you call anyone.

## When to stop and call an engineer

If you've cleaned the filter, the hose and sink connection are clear, and the machine still won't drain, the fault is most likely inside the appliance. The table below shows what common symptoms tend to point to.

| What you notice | What it often points to |
| --- | --- |
| Humming or buzzing when it tries to drain, but no water moves | A drain pump that is jammed or failing |
| Complete silence at the drain stage | A failed pump, a wiring fault, or a control problem |
| Drains slowly, then stops with an error | A partial blockage in the internal sump hose or pump housing |
| Drains, but the drum won't spin | A worn motor, motor brushes, drive belt or door lock fault |
| Water leaking from underneath | A split hose, a perished seal or a cracked pump housing |
| Tripping the electrics, or a burning smell | Stop using the machine and keep it unplugged until it is checked |

Some of these, such as a pump replacement, need the machine partly dismantled. Others involve live electrical components and testing with a meter. At that point it's safer, and often quicker, to have an engineer diagnose it properly.

## Quick recap

1. Switch off, unplug, let the water cool and don't force the door.
2. Rule out a programme setting or an unbalanced load.
3. Drain and clean the pump filter.
4. Check the drain hose for kinks and blockages.
5. Check the sink trap or standpipe, especially after a new install.
6. Cut back on detergent if the drum is full of suds.
